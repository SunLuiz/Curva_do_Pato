42 Years of NASA POWER Trends Across Brazilian Biomes: Seasonal Mann-Kendall + Theil-Sen Attribution of Surface Solar Brightening (1984–2025)

Author: Luiz Antonio Gois Junior (Team Curva do Pato) · Platform: Cockpit-OMNI (www.curveofduck.xyz) · Source Code: github.com/SunLuiz/Curva_do_Pato (MIT)

Abstract (Answer-First Summary): Over 42 continuous years of monthly NASA POWER (MERRA-2 / CERES) observations (1984-01 to 2025-12, n = 504 months per series), Cockpit-OMNI evaluates seven Earth system variables across three contrasting Brazilian biomes using Seasonal Mann-Kendall (period = 12, α = 0.05) and deseasonalized Theil-Sen regression (95% CI). All three stations warmed significantly (Campinas-SP +0.4091 °C/dec, Petrolina-PE +0.3012 °C/dec, Manaus-AM +0.3065 °C/dec) and lost rainfall, while surface shortwave solar irradiance increased everywhere. Crucially, the exact same statistical engine delivers opposite physical causal verdicts: solar brightening in Petrolina-PE (-0.9647 %/dec cloud attenuation, p = 2.833e-09; clear-sky null p = 0.2901) and Manaus-AM (-0.8552 %/dec cloud attenuation, p = 3.015e-08; clear-sky null p = 0.7686) is cloud-driven, whereas Campinas-SP exhibits stationary clouds (p = 0.9476) alongside surging clear-sky irradiance (+0.1897 MJ/m²/day/dec, p < 1e-16), isolating an aerosol / clear-sky atmospheric transparency shift. Monthly 2m wind speed (WS2M) shows zero significant trend at any station (p = 0.4890 / 0.1734 / 0.4512) and is explicitly barred from sub-hourly ramp claims.

1. How does Cockpit-OMNI compute non-parametric climate trends without seasonal distortion?

Direct Answer: Cockpit-OMNI computes trends deterministically in both Python (pymannkendall + scipy.stats.theilslopes) and pure browser JavaScript (frontend/src/engine/trend.js) by comparing matching calendar months across 42 years (Seasonal Mann-Kendall, period = 12), removing NASA POWER annual summary rows (YYYY13) and -999.0 sentinel values prior to estimating median deseasonalized Theil-Sen slopes.

Ordinary least-squares regression fails on multi-decade climatology because single El Niño spikes skew the slope and regular summer-winter oscillations mask secular change. For example, in Campinas-SP all-sky irradiance (ALLSKY_SFC_SW_DWN), an ordinary un-seasonalized Mann-Kendall test produces p = 0.1968 (falsely labeling the series "no trend"), whereas Seasonal Mann-Kendall isolates the true decadal signal at p = 0.0008616 (τ = +0.103, +0.1621 MJ/m²/day/dec).
